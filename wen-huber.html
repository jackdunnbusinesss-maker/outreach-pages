You already know the feeling.

This isn't burnout. It's not depression. It's something quieter and more specific than that.

— You've started the project. More than once. It never gets past the first few weeks before you lose the thread and quietly shelve it.
— Everything's objectively fine. You have the job, the relationship, the life that looks right from the outside. But you feel like you're in a holding pattern, waiting for the real thing to start.
— You do creative work for other people all day — or you're in a job that has nothing to do with what you actually care about — and your own creative direction keeps getting moved to "later."
— You've tried the morning routines, the journaling, the productivity systems. They help for two weeks and then the drift comes back, because those things were solving the wrong problem.
— Part of you wonders if you're just not the kind of person who actually makes things — or if you're waiting for something you can't name to give you permission to start.

Clarity first. Then everything else gets easier.

The drift isn't a discipline problem. It's a direction problem. Here's what the 8 weeks actually look like.

1

Weeks 1-2: Get honest about what you actually want to build

Not the aspirational version. The real one. Most people arrive with a vague sense of what they want to make. We get specific. The fog usually starts lifting here — sometimes in the first session.

2

Weeks 3-4: Build the first real version of it

Not perfect. The starting version. This is the part most people skip because they're waiting until they're "ready." You won't feel ready. We build anyway.

3

Weeks 5-6: Build the structure that makes it sustainable

A creative practice that actually fits around your life — not a fantasy schedule that works for two weeks and then collapses. Specific to your situation.

4

Weeks 7-8: Review, adjust, set up to continue without me

The goal is for you to not need me after this. You'll have a clear direction, a real output, and a way of working you can sustain. That's the end state.
